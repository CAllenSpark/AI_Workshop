# Vashon Island Timeline

## Narrative History: Prehistory to Present

---

### Prehistory (~12,000–~10,000 BCE)

The Vashon Stade of the Fraser Glaciation — named after Vashon Island itself — was the last major glacial advance in the Puget Sound region. As the ice sheet retreated between 12,000 and 10,000 years ago, it carved the deep waterways of Puget Sound and left behind the glacial till that forms the island's bluffs, ravines, and clay soils. Archaeological evidence indicates human presence on the island dating back 10,000 to 12,000 years, among the earliest in the Puget Sound region.

### Indigenous Era (~1500–1850s)

The sx̌ʷəbabš (Swiftwater People), also known as the Sqababsh or S'Homamish, established permanent settlements on Vashon and Maury Islands approximately 500 years ago. They built five main village sites and were part of the broader Coast Salish cultural group, relying on salmon fishing, shellfish gathering, hunting, and plant harvesting. The Suquamish and other tribes also used the islands as seasonal encampments. The Marpole culture had a presence dating back roughly 7,000 years.

### European Exploration (1792–1841)

In 1792, Captain George Vancouver named the island after his friend Captain James Vashon of the Royal Navy during British exploratory missions charting Puget Sound. In 1841, Lt. Charles Wilkes of the United States Exploring Expedition surveyed the area and named Maury Island after crew member William A. Maury. At this time, the two islands were connected only by a natural sandbar visible at low tide.

### Logging & Treaty Era (1850s)

Commercial logging began on Vashon Island in 1852, exploiting dense old-growth forests of Douglas fir, western red cedar, and hemlock. The Medicine Creek Treaty of 1854, negotiated by Territorial Governor Isaac Stevens, ceded indigenous land claims to the U.S. government. By 1855–1856, the S'Homamish people were relocated to Fox Island, ending thousands of years of continuous habitation and opening the island to European-American homesteading.

### Pioneer Settlement (1865–1890)

Mathew Bridges filed the first homestead claim on Vashon Island in 1865. Early settlers relied on fishing and logging. By the 1880s, enough people had arrived to warrant semi-regular ship service — S.D. Sherman purchased a lifeboat and refitted it as a schooner. Ferry service expanded to both the north and south ends of the island. The community built its first school, general stores, and crude roads.

### Growth & Industry (1890s)

The decade brought rapid development. Japanese Americans began growing strawberries around 1890, establishing what would become the island's signature agricultural industry. A drydock was constructed at Dockton on Maury Island — the only one of its kind on Puget Sound. Vashon College opened near Burton in 1892, with faculty member Oliver Scott Van Olinda becoming the community's first historian. The decade also brought hardship: 1893 saw both a nationwide economic collapse and a devastating forest fire in the island's center.

### Early 20th Century (1900–1920s)

Population doubled from 944 in 1900 to 2,810 in 1920. Denichiro Mukai arrived in 1910 and developed a renowned strawberry barreling method. In 1913, a permanent tombolo was constructed connecting Vashon and Maury Islands year-round. The first auto ferry arrived in 1916 when the King County Ferry System began running the M.V. Vashon between Des Moines and Portage.

### WWII Era (1940s)

Following the attack on Pearl Harbor and Executive Order 9066, Vashon Island's Japanese American community was forcibly relocated to internment camps in 1942. This devastated the strawberry industry that had been a cornerstone of the island economy for 50 years. Most families lost their land and property, and few returned after the war.

### State Ferry Era (1950s)

Washington State Ferries assumed control of island ferry service on June 1, 1951. State officials proposed connecting Vashon-Maury Island to the mainland with bridges, but islanders increasingly opposed the idea, recognizing it would destroy the island's rural character.

### Modern Era (1960s–Present)

Vashon Island has deliberately preserved its rural isolation by resisting bridge construction. The island lost two major industrial employers: K2 Sports moved manufacturing to China, and Seattle's Best Coffee's roastery closed after acquisition by Starbucks. The island's population was 11,055 at the 2020 census. Today, Vashon is known for organic farming, the arts community, and the annual Strawberry Festival celebrating its agricultural heritage.
