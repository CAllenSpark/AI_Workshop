# Vashon Island — Geography, Ecology & Climate

## Geology

### Glacial Origins
Vashon Island was formed by the retreat of the Vashon Stade of the Fraser Glaciation, the last major ice age in the Puget Sound region (~12,000–10,000 BCE). The island's terrain is composed of glacial till — a mix of clay, sand, gravel, and boulders deposited by the retreating ice sheet. The glaciation carved the deep waterways of Puget Sound that now surround the island.

### Terrain
- **Bluffs:** Steep glacial bluffs line much of the coastline, particularly on the western and northern shores
- **Ravines:** Deep forested ravines cut through the island's interior, created by stream erosion through glacial deposits
- **Soils:** Rich glacial soils support agriculture, particularly the clay loam that proved ideal for strawberry cultivation
- **Elevation:** Rolling terrain with moderate elevation; highest point approximately 450 feet above sea level

## Ecology

### Historic Forest Cover
Before European-American settlement, the island was densely forested with old-growth:
- **Douglas fir** (Pseudotsuga menziesii) — dominant canopy species
- **Western red cedar** (Thuja plicata) — culturally significant to Coast Salish peoples
- **Western hemlock** (Tsuga heterophylla) — shade-tolerant understory
- **Big-leaf maple** (Acer macrophyllum) — in ravines and lowlands
- **Red alder** (Alnus rubra) — pioneer species in disturbed areas

Logging beginning in 1852 and the devastating 1893 fire significantly altered the forest composition. Today, second-growth forest covers much of the undeveloped interior.

### Marine Environment
- **Shoreline:** Mix of rocky beaches, sandy shores, and tidal flats
- **Marine life:** Rich Puget Sound waters support salmon (multiple species), shellfish (clams, oysters, mussels), Dungeness crab, and diverse fish populations
- **Eelgrass beds:** Nearshore eelgrass provides critical habitat for juvenile fish and invertebrates
- **Marine mammals:** Harbor seals, occasional orca whale sightings in surrounding waters

### Agricultural Landscape
- **Strawberry farming:** Signature crop from 1890 to 1942 (Japanese American era)
- **Organic farming:** Modern era emphasis; island known for organic and sustainable agriculture
- **Orchards:** Historic apple and pear orchards
- **Pasture:** Small-scale livestock farming

## Climate

### Pacific Maritime
Vashon Island has a mild, maritime climate moderated by the waters of Puget Sound:
- **Winters:** Cool and wet (average January temperature ~40°F / 4°C)
- **Summers:** Warm and dry (average July temperature ~65°F / 18°C)
- **Precipitation:** ~38 inches annually, concentrated October through March
- **Rain shadow:** Partially in the Olympic rain shadow, receiving less rain than areas to the west
- **Frost:** Occasional; the maritime influence moderates temperature extremes
- **Growing season:** Approximately 200–250 days, favorable for agriculture

## Environmental Timeline

| Period | Environmental State |
|--------|-------------------|
| Pre-12000 BCE | Covered by Vashon glaciation ice sheet |
| ~10000 BCE | Glacial retreat; bare terrain, early succession |
| ~10000 BCE–1850s | Old-growth forest; rich marine ecosystem; indigenous stewardship |
| 1852–1890s | Active logging; forest clearance begins |
| 1893 | Major wildfire burns central island forests |
| 1890–1942 | Agricultural conversion; strawberry fields, orchards |
| 1942–present | Mixed second-growth forest; organic farming; conservation efforts |
