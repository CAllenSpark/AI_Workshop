# Vashon Island — Key Personalities

## Indigenous Peoples

### sx̌ʷəbabš (Sqababsh / S'Homamish)
- **Who:** The Swiftwater People, a Coast Salish group
- **Period:** ~1500–1856
- **Significance:** Original permanent inhabitants of Vashon and Maury Islands. Built five main village sites. Relied on salmon fishing, shellfish, hunting, and plant harvesting. Descendants are enrolled members of the Puyallup Tribe of Indians.
- **Related entries:** ind-001, log-002

### Lucy Gerand
- **Who:** Puyallup woman and vital historical witness to pre-settlement Vashon Island life
- **Period:** Early 1800s–1929
- **Significance:** Interviewed by anthropologist T.T. Waterman in the 1920s, she provided detailed accounts of traditional place names, village sites, and seasonal resource gathering patterns of the sx̌ʷəbabš (S'Homamish) people on Vashon and Maury Islands. Her testimony remains one of the most important primary sources for understanding indigenous lifeways on the island.
- **Related entries:** ind-009

## Archaeologists and Researchers

### Julie K. Stein
- **Who:** Archaeologist and editor; University of Washington professor
- **Period:** 1990s–2000s
- **Significance:** Co-edited the publication "Vashon Island Archaeology: A View from Burton Acres Shell Midden" (2003), documenting the collaborative excavation of the Burton Acres Shell Midden site after a 1995 storm exposed its contents.
- **Related entries:** ind-003

### Laura S. Phillips
- **Who:** Archaeologist and editor
- **Period:** 1990s–2000s
- **Significance:** Co-edited "Vashon Island Archaeology: A View from Burton Acres Shell Midden" (2003) with Julie K. Stein, publishing the findings from the 1995 excavation involving 375 volunteers.
- **Related entries:** ind-003

### Judy Wright
- **Who:** Researcher involved in the Burton Acres Shell Midden excavation
- **Period:** 1990s
- **Significance:** Contributed to the collaborative excavation and study of the Burton Acres Shell Midden on Quartermaster Harbor, which documented centuries of indigenous habitation.
- **Related entries:** ind-003

## Explorers

### Manuel Quimper
- **Who:** Peruvian-born Spanish naval explorer
- **Period:** Active on the Strait of Juan de Fuca in 1790
- **Significance:** Sailed the Princesa Real into the Strait of Juan de Fuca in 1790, discovering Admiralty Inlet -- the entrance to Puget Sound -- but did not enter. First European to report seeing Mount Baker.
- **Related entries:** exp-003

### Juan Carrasco
- **Who:** Spanish naval pilot serving under Manuel Quimper
- **Period:** Active 1790
- **Significance:** Identified the entrance to Admiralty Inlet (leading to Puget Sound and the waters around Vashon Island) during the 1790 Spanish expedition, though the expedition did not proceed south into it.
- **Related entries:** exp-003

### Francisco de Eliza
- **Who:** Spanish naval commander at Nootka Sound
- **Period:** Active 1790–1791
- **Significance:** Ordered both the 1790 Quimper expedition and the 1791 Narvaez expedition to explore the Strait of Juan de Fuca. The 1791 expedition passed by Admiralty Inlet but did not enter Puget Sound, leaving it for British discovery the following year.
- **Related entries:** exp-003, exp-004

### Jose Maria Narvaez
- **Who:** Spanish naval pilot sailing under Francisco de Eliza
- **Period:** Active 1791
- **Significance:** Passed by Admiralty Inlet in 1791 aboard the Santa Saturnina but returned to Nootka Sound without exploring Puget Sound, leaving the waterway for George Vancouver's expedition the next year.
- **Related entries:** exp-004

### Captain George Vancouver
- **Who:** British Royal Navy officer and explorer
- **Period:** Active on Puget Sound in 1792
- **Significance:** Named Vashon Island after his friend Captain James Vashon during British surveys of Puget Sound. Produced the first European maps of the area.
- **Related entries:** exp-001, exp-005

### Captain James Vashon
- **Who:** British Royal Navy officer
- **Period:** Contemporary of Vancouver
- **Significance:** The island's namesake. A fellow naval officer and friend of George Vancouver.
- **Related entries:** exp-001

### Peter Puget
- **Who:** Lieutenant aboard HMS Discovery under Captain George Vancouver
- **Period:** Active on Puget Sound in May 1792
- **Significance:** Led a week-long survey of the southern Puget Sound in two rowing craft, passing Vashon Island and charting the waterway's farthest inlets. Vancouver named the southern portion of the waterway "Puget's Sound" in his honor on May 29, 1792.
- **Related entries:** exp-005

### Joseph Whidbey
- **Who:** Master aboard HMS Discovery under Captain George Vancouver
- **Period:** Active on Puget Sound in May 1792
- **Significance:** Accompanied Peter Puget on the week-long survey of southern Puget Sound, departing HMS Discovery on May 20, 1792, and sailing past Vashon Island while charting the waterway.
- **Related entries:** exp-005

### William A. Maury
- **Who:** Member of the United States Exploring Expedition crew under Lt. Charles Wilkes
- **Period:** Active 1841
- **Significance:** Maury Island's namesake. The smaller island adjacent to Vashon was named after him when Lt. Wilkes charted the waters of Puget Sound in 1841.
- **Related entries:** exp-002

### Lt. Charles Wilkes
- **Who:** U.S. Navy officer commanding the United States Exploring Expedition (1838–1842)
- **Period:** Surveyed Puget Sound in 1841
- **Significance:** Named Maury Island after crew member William A. Maury. Named Quartermaster Harbor and nearly all of Vashon-Maury Island's coastal points after quartermasters on his voyage. First American survey of the area.
- **Related entries:** exp-002, exp-007

### Archibald McDonald
- **Who:** Chief Trader for the Hudson's Bay Company
- **Period:** Active 1833
- **Significance:** Established Fort Nisqually in 1833, the first European settlement on Puget Sound, near the mouth of Sequalitchew Creek. The fort served as a fur trade hub and drew European and American attention to the broader Puget Sound region, including Vashon Island.
- **Related entries:** exp-006

## Treaty Era / Hudson's Bay Company

### William Tolmie
- **Who:** Chief Factor at the Hudson's Bay Company's Fort Nisqually
- **Period:** Active 1843–1860s
- **Significance:** Managed the Puget Sound Agricultural Company from Fort Nisqually, running livestock operations that competed with American settlers for land. His presence and the HBC's claims complicated early American settlement patterns around Vashon Island as the British and American governments negotiated sovereignty over the Oregon Country.
- **Related entries:** log-010

### Samuel Royal Thurston
- **Who:** Oregon Territory delegate to the U.S. Congress
- **Period:** 1850s
- **Significance:** Authored the Donation Land Claim Act of 1850, which granted up to 640 acres of Oregon Territory land to married settlers. The act provided the legal mechanism under which Vashon Island was later claimed, starting in the 1860s. It explicitly excluded Black and Hawaiian settlers.
- **Related entries:** log-003

### Isaac Stevens
- **Who:** First Governor of Washington Territory
- **Period:** 1850s
- **Significance:** Negotiated the Medicine Creek Treaty of 1854, which ceded indigenous lands to the U.S. government and led to the removal of the S'Homamish from Vashon Island. His treaty terms provoked the Puget Sound Indian War of 1855–1856.
- **Related entries:** log-002, log-005

### Chief Leschi
- **Who:** Nisqually tribal leader
- **Period:** 1850s
- **Significance:** Led resistance against the Treaty of Medicine Creek's unfair terms during the Puget Sound Indian War of 1855–1856. Retreated east after the Battle of Seattle (January 26, 1856), was later captured, controversially convicted of murder, and hanged in 1858. Posthumously exonerated in 2004.
- **Related entries:** log-005

### Leschi's Wife (Mary)
- **Who:** Wife of Chief Leschi of the Nisqually
- **Period:** Active 1850s
- **Significance:** During the Puget Sound War, she and other family members were among those displaced by the conflict. Her story represents the broader experience of indigenous women and families during the treaty era and forced relocations.
- **Related entries:** log-005

### Leschi's Wife (Mary)
- **Who:** Wife of Chief Leschi of the Nisqually
- **Period:** Active 1850s
- **Significance:** During the Puget Sound War, she and other family members were among those displaced by the conflict. Her story represents the broader experience of indigenous women and families during the treaty era and forced relocations.
- **Related entries:** log-005

### John Swan
- **Who:** U.S. military official overseeing Fox Island internment
- **Period:** 1855–1856
- **Significance:** Served as guard over nearly 5,000 indigenous men, women, and children -- including the S'Homamish from Vashon Island -- who were forcibly interned on Fox Island during the Puget Sound Indian War. Over one-third of the internees fell ill under devastating conditions.
- **Related entries:** log-005

## Logging Era

### Andrew J. Pope
- **Who:** Co-principal of the Puget Mill Company at Port Gamble
- **Period:** Active 1864
- **Significance:** Together with William C. Talbot, filed the first formal land claim on Vashon Island on August 1, 1864 -- 80 acres on the west side of the island for logging operations. Both were from Maine sawmill families.
- **Related entries:** log-006

### William C. Talbot
- **Who:** Co-principal of the Puget Mill Company at Port Gamble
- **Period:** Active 1864
- **Significance:** Together with Andrew J. Pope, filed the first non-indigenous land claim on Vashon Island in 1864. Their Port Gamble mill had been operating since 1853, and the Vashon claim expanded their timber operations throughout Puget Sound.
- **Related entries:** log-006

### Arthur Phinney
- **Who:** Early land claimant on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active 1864
- **Significance:** Made land claims totaling 592 acres on Vashon Island in 1864, the same year Pope and Talbot filed their claim. His claims encompassed the area that would later become the town of Vashon.
- **Related entries:** log-006

## Pioneers

### Mathew Bridges
- **Who:** First European-American settler on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Filed homestead claim in 1865
- **Significance:** First permanent homesteader, marking the beginning of European-American settlement on the island.
- **Related entries:** pio-001

### Mary Bridges
- **Who:** Wife of Mathew Bridges, the first homesteader
- **Period:** Active 1860s–1870s
- **Significance:** Believed to have been of Duwamish descent, representing one of the earliest examples of intermarriage between settlers and indigenous peoples on the island. Her presence highlights the complex relationships between indigenous and settler communities in the earliest days of homesteading.
- **Related entries:** pio-001

### Salmon Sherman
- **Who:** Early pioneer and family settler on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Arrived 1877
- **Significance:** Along with his wife Eliza Sherman, settled along Quartermaster Harbor in 1877, taking shelter in an abandoned logger's shack they nicknamed "Fort Necessity." Considered the first permanent family settlers. Descendants, now in their sixth generation, still live on the island.
- **Related entries:** pio-004

### Eliza Sherman
- **Who:** Pioneer settler; wife of Salmon Sherman
- **Period:** Arrived 1877
- **Significance:** Settled with her husband Salmon Sherman along Quartermaster Harbor in 1877. Part of the first permanent family settlement on Vashon Island, arriving with the Price and Gilman families from Minnesota.
- **Related entries:** pio-004

### S.D. Sherman
- **Who:** Early island entrepreneur and mail carrier
- **Period:** Active ~1880s
- **Significance:** Established the first semi-regular ferry service to Vashon Island by converting a lifeboat into the schooner Old Black Joe. Previously rowed mail from Tacoma to Quartermaster Harbor. Served as an essential transportation link for the growing island community.
- **Related entries:** pio-002, pio-004, pio-006

### T.D. Soper
- **Who:** Early island settler and road petitioner
- **Period:** Active 1883
- **Significance:** Petitioned King County for Road No. 117 (today's SW Bank Road), leading from the island's center to the brickyard and post office at Vashon Landing. This was among the first official county roads on the island.
- **Related entries:** pio-005

### Edwin Broadway
- **Who:** Early island settler and road petitioner
- **Period:** Active 1883
- **Significance:** Petitioned King County for Road No. 116 (today's SW Cemetery Road), running from the center of the island to the Ellisport dock. One of the first official county roads established on Vashon Island.
- **Related entries:** pio-005

### Mason Holmes
- **Who:** Early island settler and road petitioner
- **Period:** Active 1883
- **Significance:** Petitioned King County for Road No. 118 (today's Vashon Highway SW), running south from Road No. 117. This road became one of the island's most important transportation arteries.
- **Related entries:** pio-005

### Samuel C. Herriott
- **Who:** First postmaster on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Appointed April 12, 1883
- **Significance:** Served as the first postmaster when the Vashon Post Office opened on April 12, 1883, establishing an official U.S. government presence and regularizing mail delivery on the island.
- **Related entries:** pio-006

### Albert Atwood
- **Who:** Methodist minister
- **Period:** Active 1884
- **Significance:** Organized the first Methodist class on Vashon Island on April 4, 1884, leading to the construction of the island's first church building the following year.
- **Related entries:** pio-007

### John N. Dennison
- **Who:** Representative of Seattle's Methodist Church
- **Period:** Active 1884
- **Significance:** Sent Rev. Albert Atwood to organize a Methodist class on Vashon Island in response to requests from island residents, initiating the establishment of the island's first church.
- **Related entries:** pio-007

### R.B. Dilworth
- **Who:** Methodist minister and Chautauqua organizer
- **Period:** Active 1885
- **Significance:** Organized the first Chautauqua assembly on Puget Sound, held at Dilworth Point on Vashon Island on August 3, 1885. Had been active in the original Chautauqua assembly in New York since 1874. Dilworth Point is named after him.
- **Related entries:** pio-008

### John F. Vanderhoef
- **Who:** Steamboat captain and Mosquito Fleet operator
- **Period:** Active 1887 onward
- **Significance:** Took command of the steamer Iola in 1887 and established six weekly round trips between Tacoma, Seattle, and Vashon Island landings, creating the island's first reliable Mosquito Fleet connection. Held the mail contract for the east side of Vashon-Maury Island.
- **Related entries:** pio-009

### Lucian Cook
- **Who:** Brickyard operator and island entrepreneur
- **Period:** Active ~1888–1900
- **Significance:** Ran a brickyard on Quartermaster Harbor, bought the steamer Estella to transport bricks and workmen to Tacoma, and later commissioned six barges and purchased the steamer Seaside as a towboat. Key figure in the island's brick manufacturing boom that supplied Seattle and Tacoma.
- **Related entries:** pio-010, gro-003

### Frank Gorsuch
- **Who:** Pioneer merchant and town founder
- **Period:** Active 1890
- **Significance:** Built and opened the first store at the crossroads that became the town of Vashon in 1890. His general store, which still stands, effectively founded the town of Vashon. The building was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2000.
- **Related entries:** pio-011

### Ira Thompson
- **Who:** Island pioneer
- **Period:** Active 1890s
- **Significance:** Recalled that "although overland travel was tough and the mud deep, Gorsuch was soon doing business with people throughout the Island," providing firsthand testimony about the founding of the town of Vashon.
- **Related entries:** pio-011

### Miles F. Hatch (also Miles Hatch)
- **Who:** Island entrepreneur, town founder, and college benefactor
- **Period:** Active 1890s–1900s
- **Significance:** Founded and named the town of Burton after his birthplace. Donated the hilltop campus land for Vashon College and spearheaded its founding in 1892. Operated a sawmill at Assembly Point, developed the Mileta Farm dairy, and built a store at the Burton Peninsula. Burton became the island's dominant town under his leadership.
- **Related entries:** pio-012, gro-005, gro-010, gro-012

### A.C. Jones
- **Who:** First president of Vashon College
- **Period:** Active 1892
- **Significance:** Hired as the first president of Vashon College at Burton, which opened in 1892 as a non-denominational Christian co-educational institution offering classical and scientific programs, a pharmacy school, conservatory of music, and school of commerce.
- **Related entries:** pio-012, gro-005

## Growth Era

### John Cage Gorsuch
- **Who:** Pioneer farmer and commercial strawberry grower
- **Period:** Active ~1893–1896
- **Significance:** Planted the first commercial strawberry operation on Vashon Island in the early 1890s. By 1896, he was harvesting 6,120 pounds per acre from his three-acre farm, launching the island's signature agricultural industry that would ship 120,000 crates annually by 1909.
- **Related entries:** gro-009

### Howard C. Stone
- **Who:** Local carpenter
- **Period:** Active 1894
- **Significance:** Built the two-story Burton Masonic Hall for the Woodmen of the World fraternal order in 1894, the island's first fraternal building. The hall is now a designated King County landmark.
- **Related entries:** gro-007

### J.E. Mace
- **Who:** Island pioneer and beachcomber
- **Period:** Active 1895
- **Significance:** Discovered bags of smuggled opium on a Vashon Island beach in 1895 and reported his find to the Seattle Customs Office. Received $175 by court order for turning in the opium, revealing the island's position along Pacific Northwest maritime smuggling routes.
- **Related entries:** gro-008

### Asa Start
- **Who:** Island resident
- **Period:** Active 1895
- **Significance:** Neighbor of J.E. Mace, on whose property Mace buried smuggled opium found on the beach in 1895 before reporting it to government authorities.
- **Related entries:** gro-008

### J.T. Thompson
- **Who:** Early island resident
- **Period:** Active 1891
- **Significance:** The first telephone line on Vashon Island ran half a mile between the local newspaper office and the J.T. Thompson household in 1891 -- little more than an acoustic wire system.
- **Related entries:** gro-011

### Frances Sherman
- **Who:** Sawmill operator
- **Period:** Active ~1890s–1900s
- **Significance:** Ran a sawmill in Paradise Valley and won a contract to supply lumber for the Luna Park amusement park at Alki Point. Part of the island's active lumber industry during the growth era.
- **Related entries:** gro-012

### Helmar Steen
- **Who:** Mill operator and logging entrepreneur
- **Period:** Active ~1902 onward
- **Significance:** Founded the Steen Mill and built a logging railroad along what is now Cove Road. His mill provided his home with an early luxury -- electric light -- before the island's formal electrification in 1913.
- **Related entries:** gro-012

### John A. Martinolich
- **Who:** Croatian-born shipbuilder, known as the "King of Purse Seiners"
- **Period:** Active 1910s–1940s
- **Significance:** Operated the Martinolich Shipyard at Dockton on Quartermaster Harbor, building and repairing vessels for the Puget Sound fishing fleet. His yard attracted a community of Croatian workers, creating a distinctive ethnic enclave at Dockton that locals called "Ichville." His purse seiners were considered the finest on Puget Sound.
- **Related entries:** gro-013

### Alfred J. Stuckey
- **Who:** Superintendent of the Martinolich Shipyard (Dockton Dry Dock)
- **Period:** Active 1892–1909
- **Significance:** Oversaw construction of numerous vessels including steam launches and small freighters. Under his management, the yard employed over 80 workers and became one of the most important maritime industrial operations on Puget Sound.
- **Related entries:** gro-003

### Peter Manson
- **Who:** Dockton village founder and marine construction pioneer
- **Period:** Active 1890s–1920s
- **Significance:** Named the village of Dockton on Quartermaster Harbor, noting the dry dock facility could "dock a ton of boats." Founded a pile driving company that grew into Manson Construction, which became a major marine construction firm still operating today. His entrepreneurship at Dockton exemplified the industrial ambition of Vashon's growth era.
- **Related entries:** gro-003

### Mark Twain
- **Who:** American author (Samuel Clemens)
- **Period:** Reportedly spoke at Vashon Chautauqua, late 1800s–early 1900s
- **Significance:** Reportedly among the notable speakers at the Puget Sound Chautauqua Assembly at Tramp Harbor on Vashon Island, which featured a 1,200-seat pavilion and drew audiences from across the region.
- **Related entries:** gro-004

### Theodore Roosevelt
- **Who:** 26th President of the United States
- **Period:** Reportedly spoke at Vashon Chautauqua, early 1900s
- **Significance:** Reportedly among the notable speakers at the Puget Sound Chautauqua Assembly at Tramp Harbor on Vashon Island, reflecting the assembly's national cultural significance.
- **Related entries:** gro-004

## Agricultural Leaders

### Denichiro Mukai
- **Who:** Japanese American farmer and innovator
- **Period:** Arrived 1910; active through 1942 (internment)
- **Significance:** Developed a renowned strawberry barreling method that concentrated flavor and moisture. Built a cold-process fruit barreling plant in 1926. His farmstead, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1994, is now a heritage site. Was among those forcibly interned during WWII.
- **Related entries:** ear-001, ear-012, ww2-001, mod-007

### Kuni Mukai
- **Who:** Japanese immigrant; second wife of Denichiro Mukai
- **Period:** Active 1920s–1990s
- **Significance:** Designed and installed the formal Japanese stroll garden at Mukai Farm in the 1930s -- the only known Japanese garden of that era designed by a woman. Self-exiled to Oregon with the family during WWII to avoid internment. Returned to Vashon after the war and resumed farming.
- **Related entries:** ear-012, ww2-003, ww2-006, mod-007

### Masahiro (Masa) Mukai
- **Who:** American-born son of Denichiro Mukai; farmer and inventor
- **Period:** Born 1911 on Vashon Island; active through 1990s
- **Significance:** Because his parents could not own land under Washington's 1921 Alien Land Law, the family purchased 40 acres in Masa's name in 1926. Designed the cold-process fruit barreling plant as a teenager. Received advance warning of internment from a military friend and moved his family to eastern Oregon, making them the only Vashon Japanese Americans to avoid the concentration camps. Invented his own seed harvester in Oregon. Returned to Vashon after the war.
- **Related entries:** ear-012, ww2-003, ww2-006

### Sato Mukai
- **Who:** First wife of Denichiro Mukai
- **Period:** Active 1910–1921
- **Significance:** Arrived on Vashon with Denichiro as part of the early Japanese American farming community. Her death in 1921 preceded the family's strategic land purchase in their American-born son Masa's name in 1926, a legal maneuver necessitated by the Washington State Alien Land Law that barred Japanese immigrants from owning property.
- **Related entries:** ear-001

### Deb Harrington
- **Who:** Local contractor on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active 1926
- **Significance:** Constructed the Mukai Cold Process Fruit Barreling Plant in 1926, a one-story wood-frame building designed by young Masahiro Mukai. The plant is now a King County landmark and National Register site.
- **Related entries:** ear-012

### Melissa Jaynes
- **Who:** Island elder and first Strawberry Queen
- **Period:** Active 1923
- **Significance:** Crowned Vashon's first Strawberry Queen in 1923 at the age of 101, during the annual Strawberry Festival that celebrated the island's booming berry industry.
- **Related entries:** ear-005

## Early 20th Century

### Lewis Cass Beall Sr
- **Who:** Founder of the Harrington-Beall Greenhouse Company
- **Period:** Active from ~1888
- **Significance:** Founded the Harrington-Beall Greenhouse Company on a 23-acre site southeast of Vashon's business center. The business grew into the nation's largest grower of roses and an orchid establishment of international renown, operating for 101 years.
- **Related entries:** ear-004

### Tom Beall Jr
- **Who:** Grandson of Lewis Cass Beall Sr; greenhouse operator
- **Period:** Active early–mid 20th century
- **Significance:** Partnered in the Harrington-Beall Greenhouse Company, growing it into a 150,000-square-foot operation with over 50 greenhouses. By 1923, it was the island's largest employer with 25 to 30 year-round workers. During WWII, rare orchids from England were shipped to the Vashon greenhouses for safekeeping.
- **Related entries:** ear-004

### Charles A. Cook
- **Who:** Tacoma resident and resort developer
- **Period:** Active 1902–1920s
- **Significance:** Platted the Magnolia Beach resort community on Vashon Island in 1902, part of the island's flourishing summer tourism industry that drew over 8,000 visitors per season from Tacoma by 1915.
- **Related entries:** ear-008

### G.A. Collins
- **Who:** Electrical power entrepreneur
- **Period:** Active 1913
- **Significance:** Built a steam electric generating plant at Ellisport, bringing the first electric lights to Vashon Island. The system was inaugurated on November 24, 1913, with Governor Ernest Lister pressing the button.
- **Related entries:** ear-007

### Ernest Lister
- **Who:** Governor of Washington State
- **Period:** Active 1913
- **Significance:** Inaugurated the Vashon Island electrical system on November 24, 1913, by pressing the button that activated G.A. Collins's steam electric plant at Ellisport.
- **Related entries:** ear-007

### Ruth A. Brown
- **Who:** Camp Fire Girls leader and camp founder
- **Period:** Active 1920–1921
- **Significance:** Founded Camp Sealth in 1920, the first permanent resident camp for girls in Washington State. The camp began on Blake Island and moved to a 150-acre site on Vashon Island's southwest corner in 1921. It has operated for over 100 continuous summers and expanded to nearly 400 acres.
- **Related entries:** ear-010

### Captain Gertrude Wiman
- **Who:** First woman licensed as a steamship pilot on Puget Sound
- **Period:** Active ~1907
- **Significance:** Earned her license around 1907 and commanded vessels on the Mosquito Fleet routes that served Vashon Island, breaking gender barriers in the maritime industry at a time when women captains were virtually unheard of. Her achievement reflected the independent spirit of island communities that depended on these small ferry operators.
- **Related entries:** ear-006

### Louis Modaranga
- **Who:** Filipino farmworker on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active 1930
- **Significance:** Attacked on the night of May 12, 1930, by four white men while employed at the Steen Mill box factory in Ellisport. Despite testimony that the assault was provoked, Modaranga was charged with felony assault while none of his attackers were arrested. His case exemplified the anti-Filipino violence faced by farmworkers during the Great Depression.
- **Related entries:** ear-013

## Historians

### Marjorie Rose Stanley
- **Who:** Vashon Island historian and documentarian
- **Period:** Active 1920s–1980s
- **Significance:** Spent over 60 years documenting island history. Published the first "History of Vashon Island" in 1926 and later serialized an expanded version in the Vashon-Maury Island Beachcomber in 1967. Her work preserved the oral histories and pioneer stories that might otherwise have been lost, forming the foundation for later historians like Bruce Haulman.
- **Related entries:** mod-015

### Oliver Scott Van Olinda (also Oliver Van Olinda)
- **Who:** Faculty member at Vashon College; community historian
- **Period:** 1890s onward
- **Significance:** Became the community's first historian, documenting early island life and settlement patterns. Taught stenography and typing on the founding faculty of Vashon College and also edited the weekly Vashon Island Press. Described the first automobiles arriving on the island in 1907 as "the most powerful influence" on island life alongside the telephone.
- **Related entries:** pio-003, gro-005, ear-011

## WWII Era — Matsuda Family

### Heisuke Matsuda
- **Who:** Japanese immigrant strawberry farmer on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active from 1927; forcibly removed 1942
- **Significance:** Farmed strawberries on Vashon Island with his wife Mitsuno. Father of Mary Matsuda Gruenewald and Yoneichi Matsuda. Forcibly removed from the island on May 16, 1942, and sent to internment camps.
- **Related entries:** ww2-002, mod-010

### Mitsuno Matsuda
- **Who:** Japanese immigrant; wife of Heisuke Matsuda
- **Period:** Active from 1927; forcibly removed 1942
- **Significance:** Strawberry farmer on Vashon Island. Mother of Mary Matsuda Gruenewald and Yoneichi Matsuda. Forcibly removed from the island and interned during WWII. Her family's story is told in her daughter's memoir "Looking Like the Enemy."
- **Related entries:** ww2-002, mod-010

### Yoneichi Matsuda
- **Who:** Japanese American soldier; son of Heisuke and Mitsuno Matsuda
- **Period:** Active 1940s
- **Significance:** Volunteered from Heart Mountain internment camp to serve in the U.S. Army's 442nd Regimental Combat Team, the most decorated unit for its size and length of service in U.S. military history. His family had farmed strawberries on Vashon Island since 1927.
- **Related entries:** ww2-002, ww2-005, mod-010

### Mary Matsuda Gruenewald
- **Who:** Author, nurse, and internment survivor; daughter of Heisuke and Mitsuno Matsuda
- **Period:** Born 1925 on Vashon Island; died 2021
- **Significance:** Published "Looking Like the Enemy: My Story of Imprisonment in Japanese American Internment Camps" in 2005 at age 80, preserving the story of Vashon's Japanese American community. Enrolled in the U.S. Cadet Nurse Corps during the war. In 2017, Vashon Island High School retroactively awarded her the diploma she was denied when internment cut short her education.
- **Related entries:** ww2-002, ww2-005, mod-010

## WWII Era — Japanese American Farming Families

### Sakai Family
- **Who:** Japanese American farming family on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active early 1900s–1942
- **Significance:** One of several prominent Japanese American families who cultivated strawberries and other crops on Vashon from the early 1900s through 1942. Like the Mukai and Matsuda families, the Sakais were forcibly removed during Executive Order 9066 and sent to internment camps. Their farms were never fully recovered after the war.
- **Related entries:** ww2-001, ww2-008

### Hoshi Family
- **Who:** Japanese American farming family on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active early 1900s–1942
- **Significance:** Part of the thriving agricultural community that made Vashon Island one of the leading strawberry producers in Washington state. Forcibly removed during the 1942 internment of Japanese Americans. Their story is part of the broader narrative of loss and resilience that shaped the island's WWII history.
- **Related entries:** ww2-001, ww2-008

### August M. Takatsuka
- **Who:** Vashon-born son of Japanese immigrant farmers; WWII veteran
- **Period:** Active 1945–1980s
- **Significance:** Returned from military service in WWII to resume strawberry farming on Vashon Island. Later transitioned to Christmas tree cultivation in the 1970s. His persistence in farming after the devastation of internment represented the resilience of the Japanese American agricultural community on Vashon.
- **Related entries:** ww2-008

## WWII Era — Mukai Family

### Chiyeko Mukai
- **Who:** Wife of Masahiro (Masa) Mukai
- **Period:** Active 1940s
- **Significance:** Self-exiled with her husband Masa from Vashon to Dead Ox Flat, Oregon, in March 1942 to avoid internment, as she had relatives in eastern Oregon. One of only four Mukai family members who avoided the concentration camps.

### Milton Mukai
- **Who:** Son of Masahiro and Chiyeko Mukai
- **Period:** Active 1940s
- **Significance:** Accompanied his parents and grandmother Kuni when the Mukai family self-exiled from Vashon Island to Dead Ox Flat, Oregon, in March 1942. One of the four Mukai family members who avoided internment.
- **Related entries:** ww2-003

## State Ferry Era

### Captain Alexander M. Peabody
- **Who:** President of the Black Ball Line (Puget Sound Navigation Company)
- **Period:** Active 1930s–1951
- **Significance:** Operated ferry service to Vashon Island before the state takeover. His contentious relationship with the Washington Toll Bridge Authority over fares and subsidies led to the dramatic 1951 state purchase of the Black Ball fleet for $4.97 million, creating Washington State Ferries — now the largest ferry system in the United States.
- **Related entries:** fer-006

### Homer Hadley
- **Who:** Engineer and bridge designer
- **Period:** Active 1930s–1950s
- **Significance:** Proposed a cross-sound bridge connecting Vashon Island to the mainland. Known for designing the Mercer Island floating bridge (Lacey V. Murrow Memorial Bridge). His Vashon bridge proposal sparked decades of debate about the island's future character — whether to remain ferry-dependent and rural or become connected and suburban.
- **Related entries:** fer-009

### Alex Anderson
- **Who:** Chairman of the Vashon Island anti-bridge committee
- **Period:** Active late 1950s
- **Significance:** Led the successful grassroots campaign that defeated the 1959 Cross-Sound Bridge proposal. The campaign's victory preserved the island's rural character and ferry-dependent identity, a decision that continues to define Vashon's relationship with the mainland.
- **Related entries:** fer-009

### John Cherberg
- **Who:** Lieutenant Governor of Washington State; Senate president
- **Period:** Active 1959
- **Significance:** Made the procedural ruling on March 12, 1959, that effectively killed the most serious Vashon Island bridge proposal in history. Ruled that the House bill had broadened the original scope and must return to committee. A vote to override his ruling failed 21 to 28, and the bridge was never built.
- **Related entries:** fer-003

### Fred Eernisse
- **Who:** Vashon Island artist and arts organizer
- **Period:** Active 1949 onward
- **Significance:** Co-founded the Vashon Arts League in 1949 with Norman Edson and Art Hansen, which became the oldest private nonprofit community arts organization in Washington State. The organization eventually grew into Vashon Center for the Arts.
- **Related entries:** fer-004

### Norman Edson
- **Who:** Vashon Island artist and arts organizer
- **Period:** Active 1949 onward
- **Significance:** Co-founded the Vashon Arts League in 1949, showing art created by islanders. The organization received 501(c)(3) status in 1966 and became the island's largest nonprofit arts institution.
- **Related entries:** fer-004

### Art Hansen
- **Who:** Vashon Island artist and arts organizer
- **Period:** Active 1949 onward
- **Significance:** Co-founded the Vashon Arts League in 1949 with Fred Eernisse and Norman Edson, establishing the institutional foundation for the island's arts community.
- **Related entries:** fer-004

### Shirley Speidel
- **Who:** Arts community leader on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active 1960s
- **Significance:** Helped revitalize the Vashon arts organization, finding a permanent home in the old Boy Scout Cabin at Ober Park. Instrumental in the organization receiving its 501(c)(3) nonprofit status in 1966 as Vashon Associated Arts.
- **Related entries:** fer-004

### Tony Grant
- **Who:** Young Vashon Island resident
- **Period:** Active ~1970
- **Significance:** A 17-year-old who was held down by three loggers who forcibly cut his hair during the cultural tensions surrounding the counterculture influx to Vashon Island around 1970. The incident exemplified the friction between long-time residents and the incoming counterculture movement.
- **Related entries:** fer-005

## Modern Era — Literature

### Betty MacDonald
- **Who:** Author and humorist
- **Period:** Active on Vashon 1940s–1958
- **Significance:** Author of "The Egg and I" (1945), a bestselling memoir of rural life that inspired the Ma and Pa Kettle film series. While that book was set on the Olympic Peninsula, MacDonald later lived on Vashon Island with her second husband Donald MacDonald from the 1940s through 1958. Her book "Onions in the Stew" (1955) is a witty portrait of island life, ferry commutes, and raising teenage daughters on Vashon. She also created the beloved children's series "Mrs. Piggle-Wiggle." Her presence cemented Vashon's literary reputation.
- **Related entries:** mod-016

## Modern Era — Industry

### Bill Kirschner
- **Who:** Engineer and co-founder of K2 Corporation
- **Period:** Active 1950s–1960s
- **Significance:** University of Washington engineering graduate who pioneered fiberglass ski technology. Experimented in his grandfather Otto's garage on Vashon Island before perfecting a wet-wrap fiberglass process in 1961. Co-founded K2 Corporation with his brother Don in 1962. The company became the island's largest private employer with approximately 750 workers at its peak.
- **Related entries:** mod-003

### Don Kirschner
- **Who:** Co-founder of K2 Corporation
- **Period:** Active 1962 onward
- **Significance:** Co-founded K2 Corporation on Vashon Island with his brother Bill in 1962, named for both the world's second-highest mountain and the two Kirschner brothers. The company revolutionized the ski industry with fiberglass technology.
- **Related entries:** mod-003

### Otto Kirschner
- **Who:** Grandfather of Bill and Don Kirschner
- **Period:** Active mid-20th century
- **Significance:** His garage on Vashon Island served as the original workshop where Bill Kirschner experimented with fiberglass ski prototypes in the 1950s, leading to the founding of K2 Corporation.
- **Related entries:** mod-003

### John de Groen
- **Who:** Co-founder and early leader of K2 Corporation
- **Period:** Active 1960s–1980s
- **Significance:** Partnered with the Kirschner brothers to transform K2 from a small island ski manufacturer into a major sporting goods company. K2 became Vashon's largest employer with approximately 750 workers at its peak, and its economic anchor for decades.
- **Related entries:** mod-003

### Bill Knox
- **Who:** Artist, designer, and engineer at K2 Corporation
- **Period:** Active 1960s–1990s
- **Significance:** Created the iconic color schemes for K2 skis and built the original prototype for the K2 ski boot. As a painter, he specialized in watercolor landscapes and wildlife, with work selected for U.S. postage stamps. His combination of artistic talent and engineering skill was central to K2's distinctive visual identity during its growth years.
- **Related entries:** mod-003

### Jim Stewart
- **Who:** Coffee entrepreneur and founder of Seattle's Best Coffee
- **Period:** Active 1982 onward
- **Significance:** Moved his coffee roasting operation to Vashon Island in 1982, purchasing a historic building in the town of Vashon. After winning a regional tasting competition, the company changed its name from Stewart Brothers Coffee to Seattle's Best Coffee. Starbucks acquired SBC in 2003 and closed the Vashon facility. The building was later reopened as The Vashon Island Coffee Roasterie.
- **Related entries:** mod-004

### Jean Findlay
- **Who:** Founding member of the Vashon-Maury Island Heritage Association
- **Period:** Active 1970s–1990s
- **Significance:** Instrumental in establishing what became the Vashon Heritage Museum. Led early efforts to preserve island historical records, oral histories, and artifacts, and to document pioneer-era structures before they were lost to demolition or neglect.

- **Who:** Vashon Island's preeminent modern historian

- **Significance:** Creator of vashonhistory.com, the most comprehensive online resource for island history. Author of multiple books on Vashon history and regular columnist for the Vashon-Maury Island Beachcomber. His work has synthesized scattered primary sources into accessible narratives, making island history available to researchers and the general public alike.
- **Related entries:** mod-015

## Modern Era — Activism and Community

### Bill Moyer
- **Who:** Activist and founder of the Backbone Campaign
- **Period:** Active 2003–present
- **Significance:** Fourth-generation Washingtonian and Vashon Island resident who founded the Backbone Campaign in 2003. Pioneered "artful activism" combining creative arts with environmental and social justice advocacy. Led campaigns including opposition to Lower Snake River dams for salmon and orca protection. His annual Action Camp on Vashon became a national model for activist training.
- **Related entries:** mod-013

### Glenda Pearson
- **Who:** Community organizer and land use advocate
- **Period:** Active 1980s–2000s
- **Significance:** Longtime Vashon Island community organizer who was instrumental in land use planning debates that shaped the island's identity. Advocated for rural character preservation and small-scale agriculture rather than suburban development, helping define the vision of Vashon that persists today.
- **Related entries:** mod-014

### Sharon Nelson
- **Who:** Environmentalist and grassroots organizer
- **Period:** Active 1998–2010
- **Significance:** Founded Preserve Our Islands, the grassroots organization that led the 13-year fight against Glacier Northwest's proposed gravel mine expansion on Maury Island. The battle ended in 2010 when King County purchased the 200-acre site for $36 million, creating a new county park.
- **Related entries:** mod-008

### Dow Constantine
- **Who:** King County Executive
- **Period:** Active 2010
- **Significance:** Completed the purchase of the Glacier Northwest gravel mine site on Maury Island on December 30, 2010, for $36 million. The 200-acre parcel became a county park, protecting a mile of shoreline and over 200 acres of madrone forest.
- **Related entries:** mod-008

### Jim Bristow
- **Who:** Community health leader on Vashon Island
- **Period:** Active 2020–2022
- **Significance:** Led the Vashon Medical Reserve Corps (VMRC) in a volunteer-driven pandemic response that achieved the lowest COVID-19 case rate in King County. The program delivered over 19,000 vaccine doses, conducted 7,500+ PCR tests, and was recognized by the New York Times as a model for rural communities.
- **Related entries:** mod-012

- **Period:** Active 1970s–2000s
- **Significance:** Helped establish the Vashon Island Growers Association and the Saturday Farmers Market, which became central to the island's identity as an organic agriculture community. Part of the back-to-the-land movement that drew young farmers to the island in the 1970s-80s.

- **Period:** Active 1980s–present
- **Significance:** Co-founded S&S Homestead Farm on Vashon, a model for sustainable biodynamic agriculture. Author of works on sustainable farming practices. His farm became an educational center for regenerative agriculture, hosting workshops and programs for decades.
- **Related entries:** mod-014

### Elizabeth Simpson
- **Who:** Co-founder of S&S Homestead Farm
- **Period:** Active 1980s–present
- **Significance:** Built S&S Homestead Farm with Henning Sehmsdorf into one of the Pacific Northwest's premier demonstrations of biodynamic and sustainable farming practices, hosting workshops and educational programs that influenced a generation of organic farmers on Vashon and beyond.
